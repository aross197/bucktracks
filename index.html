NOAA OVATION map · next 30–90 min

Where you are: Cumberland County (Springhill / Amherst / Oxford area) sits at the bottom-right edge of eastern North America on this polar map — far south of the green/yellow oval most nights.

The coloured ring is the aurora oval. It only reaches Nova Scotia when Kp climbs to 5–7+. When the oval expands south, the green band moves toward the Maritimes. Tonight’s oval is usually locked over northern Canada / Hudson Bay.

How to use this from Cumberland County

  • Kp below 5 — almost no chance. Stay home.
  • Kp 5–6 — possible faint glow low on the northern horizon if skies are crystal clear and dark.
  • Kp 7+ — real chance of visible colour. Worth driving to a dark northern outlook.
  • Best hours — 10 pm to 2 am Atlantic time.
  • Best spots — open northern horizon away from town lights (north of Springhill, Oxford, Amherst).
  • Moon — new or thin crescent is best. Bright moonlight kills faint aurora.
